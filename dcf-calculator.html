Cash Flow Assumptions
Initial FCF (Rs Cr) ₹1,000
What: The company's most recent full-year Free Cash Flow (Cash from Operations − Capital Expenditure), in ₹ Crores. This is the starting cash flow the model grows every year.

Where to find it: The "Cash Flow Statement" in the annual report, or the "Cash Flow" tab on screener.in / moneycontrol.com — subtract Capex from Operating Cash Flow for the latest year.
Discount Rate 12%
What: Your required rate of return / WACC — the annual return you demand for the risk of holding this stock. Future cash flows are "discounted" back at this rate.

Where to find it: Not published — you estimate it. A common rule of thumb for India: 10–14% for large, stable companies; 15–20%+ for smaller or riskier ones. Some investors just use their own personal target return.
Growth Rate (1 to 5 Yr) 15%
What: Expected annual % growth in FCF for the first 5 years — usually the company's higher-growth phase.

Where to find it: Look at the company's historical 3–5 year FCF/profit growth (screener.in shows "Compounded Profit Growth"), analyst estimates in research reports, or management's own growth guidance in earnings calls/investor presentations.
Growth Rate (6 to 10 Yr) 10%
What: Expected annual % FCF growth in years 6–10, as growth naturally slows down when the company matures. Should usually be lower than Growth Rate (1–5 Yr).

Where to find it: There's no direct source — a common approach is to fade it roughly halfway between your Yr 1-5 growth and the Terminal Growth Rate below.
Terminal Growth Rate 5%
What: The FCF growth rate assumed forever after year 10. No company can outgrow the economy forever, so this must stay modest — and always lower than the Discount Rate.

Where to find it: No source to look up — use a conservative estimate close to long-run nominal GDP growth (~4–6% for India) or long-term inflation. Avoid guessing anything above ~6-7%.
Company & Valuation Inputs
Market Capitalization (Rs Cr) ₹50,000
What: The company's total current market value = Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding, in ₹ Crores. Used here to back out the number of shares.

Where to find it: Shown directly on the stock's page on screener.in, moneycontrol.com, NSE/BSE websites, or Google Finance — usually labeled "Market Cap".
Current Share Price (Rs) ₹500
What: Today's trading price of one share of the company, used to compare against the model's intrinsic value estimate.

Where to find it: Live on NSE/BSE, your broker app (Zerodha, Groww, etc.), Google Finance, or screener.in — labeled "Current Price" / "CMP".
Net Debt (Rs Cr) ₹5,000
What: Total Debt − Cash & Cash Equivalents, in ₹ Crores. Subtracted from Enterprise Value to get Equity Value. Enter a negative number if the company holds more cash than debt (net cash).

Where to find it: The "Balance Sheet" section on screener.in / moneycontrol.com, or the annual report — sum up all borrowings, then subtract cash & investments.
Margin of Safety 20%
What: The discount you want below the calculated intrinsic value before you'd actually buy — a cushion against errors in your growth/rate assumptions.

Where to find it: A personal choice, not a market figure. Value investors commonly use 20–30%; use more (30%+) for uncertain businesses, less (10-15%) for very stable, predictable ones.

The Formula

EV Enterprise Value (intrinsic firm value)
FCF Free Cash Flow projected each year
r Discount rate (required return)

6 Intrinsic value/share = ₹21,306 Cr ÷ 42.1 Cr ≈ ₹506.
Verdict for Microintel
₹506 vs ₹950
Intrinsic value (₹506) is ~47% below the current price (₹950) — on these assumptions the model says it's overvalued. With a 25% margin of safety you'd only consider buying below ≈ ₹380. Change the growth or discount rate assumptions above and the answer can flip — that's the point of stress-testing a DCF rather than trusting one number.
